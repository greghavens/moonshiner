# Riverton Housing Heat Scan

Source label: `[Housing Heat Scan]`

Issued by: Riverton Department of Public Health

Study period: June 1 through August 31, 2025

## Method

The department placed temperature loggers in 72 occupied apartments across 18 existing multifamily buildings whose owners volunteered. The sample included 40 apartments in 10 East Ward buildings, 20 apartments in five Southbank buildings, and 12 apartments in three North Hill buildings. Loggers recorded hourly temperatures. Staff reviewed the 14 days during the study period on which the National Weather Service issued a local heat alert.

Staff also made a visual roof observation from an accessible point at each participating building. The observation was not a structural inspection and did not test roof materials, insulation, or coating compatibility.

## Observed findings

- In East Ward, 26 of 40 monitored apartments (65%) exceeded 82°F at 11:00 p.m. on at least half of the 14 heat-alert days. The median of each apartment's maximum overnight temperature was 84.8°F.
- In Southbank, 8 of 20 monitored apartments (40%) met the same late-night threshold. The median maximum overnight temperature was 81.9°F.
- In North Hill, 3 of 12 monitored apartments (25%) met the threshold. The median maximum overnight temperature was 80.7°F.
- Seven of the 10 participating East Ward buildings had dark, low-slope roofs that appeared to be in fair or good condition. This observation identifies candidates for further screening; it does not establish that any roof can safely accept a coating.

## Limitations

The buildings and residents were a voluntary convenience sample, not a random sample of Riverton housing. The small sample does not establish neighborhood-wide prevalence. Building age, floor, ventilation, air-conditioning access and use, occupancy, and roof condition may affect indoor temperature. The scan describes heat exposure but does not show that roof characteristics caused the observed temperatures.
