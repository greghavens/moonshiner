# Three-Year Multifamily Heat Options Memo

Source label: `[Options Memo]`

Prepared by: Riverton Office of Sustainability

Date: January 12, 2026

## Option A — Cool-roof lead with complementary trees

Option A models work across the 10 East Ward buildings represented in the heat scan. For planning purposes, staff modeled completion of seven roofs totaling 210,000 square feet and planting 120 trees near participating multifamily properties and adjoining public space.

The model assumes baseline monitoring and technical screening in Year 1, four roof completions and 60 tree plantings in Year 2, and three roof completions and 60 tree plantings in Year 3. It projects that the median maximum overnight indoor temperature for monitored East Ward apartments in treated buildings could decline from the observed 84.8°F baseline to 82.8°F or lower by Year 3. That temperature is a planning projection, not an observed result or guarantee.

The proposed Year 3 performance targets are:

- Roof area treated: 210,000 square feet. Baseline: 0 square feet.
- Maximum overnight indoor temperature: 82.8°F or lower for the monitored treated-building group. Baseline: 84.8°F median maximum overnight temperature in the East Ward heat-scan sample.
- Tree survival: at least 85% of the 120 trees planted under the program. Baseline: Not applicable because the program trees have not been planted.

## Option B — Tree-first approach

Option B would plant 300 trees near multifamily properties and adjoining public space over three years, with no city-supported roof treatment. Staff did not project a three-year indoor-temperature change for this option because canopy growth, shade, species, siting, weather, and maintenance would determine its effect. The option could yield longer-term shade and stormwater benefits, but the memo does not quantify them.

## Monitoring and interpretation

For either option, staff recommends retaining hourly indoor-temperature monitoring, recording weather and building characteristics, and reporting outputs separately from outcomes. If feasible, treated buildings should be compared with later-treated or untreated buildings. A before-and-after change alone would not prove causation because weather, occupancy, ventilation, air-conditioning use, and building work could also change.

All quantities, timing, and target values in this memo are planning assumptions or proposed targets unless explicitly identified as observed heat-scan findings. They are not evidence that either option has already produced results.
