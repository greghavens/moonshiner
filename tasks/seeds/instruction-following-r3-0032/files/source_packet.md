# Extreme-heat policy source packet

Use the bracketed source labels for citations in the decision memo. The packet is the complete research record for this assignment.

## [S1] Metroville Office of Resilience, 2026 Heat Exposure Assessment

- Ward 2 has 68% renter households, compared with 44% citywide.
- Ward 2's existing tree-canopy coverage is 11%, compared with 24% citywide.
- In 2025, Ward 2 recorded 42 days at or above 90 degrees Fahrenheit and 31 heat-related service requests per 10,000 residents. The citywide request rate was 17 per 10,000.
- Sensors on a Ward 2 demonstration building found that a high-reflectance roof was, on average, 7 degrees Fahrenheit cooler at the roof surface during sampled summer afternoons than its prior conventional roof. This was a single-building demonstration, not an estimate of neighborhood air-temperature change.

## [S2] U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, Heat Island Strategies summaries

- Trees and other vegetation cool their immediate surroundings through shade and evapotranspiration. Their benefits depend on placement, growth, and continued care.
- Cool roofs use reflective and emissive materials to reduce solar heat absorbed by a roof. They can lower roof-surface temperature and heat transfer into a building, but results vary with the building, roof, weather, and installation.
- The agency treats trees and cool roofs as complementary heat-island strategies; neither source summary claims that a single project produces a specified neighborhood-wide air-temperature reduction.

## [S3] Metroville Housing Department, 2026 Ward 2 Housing Stock Inventory

- Seventy-one percent of Ward 2 renter households live in buildings with five or more units.
- Sixty-three percent of Ward 2 multifamily buildings have flat or low-slope roofs that are technically suitable for the city's standard reflective-roof materials, subject to an on-site assessment.
- In a prior voluntary energy-upgrade outreach effort, 54% of contacted Ward 2 multifamily owners completed an assessment when the city supplied technical assistance and a tenant-notice template. The figure measures assessment participation, not completed construction.
- Building staff estimate that the existing contractor and inspection network could complete as many as 140 qualifying multifamily roof projects during an 18-month program, if owners participate.

## [S4] Metroville Parks Department, 2026 Right-of-Way Forestry Operations Memo

- The department has identified 1,120 plantable right-of-way sites in Ward 2. Twenty-six percent are within 50 feet of a renter-majority multifamily property.
- Young trees generally need 3 to 5 growing seasons before producing substantial shade. Larger shade benefits accrue as canopy matures.
- The department's three-year survival rate was 78% for trees receiving scheduled establishment watering and 52% for trees without it.
- With its current nursery, planting, and care capacity, the department can plant and place 420 Ward 2 trees into scheduled establishment care during an 18-month window.

## [S5] Metroville Public Health and Tenant Advisory Panel, Program Design Note

- Tenant listening sessions favored improvements that do not require an individual renter to apply or arrange installation. Participants also asked to receive advance notice of roof work and plain-language information about expected benefits and disruption.
- Cool-roof benefits are most direct for upper-floor units and buildings with roof-driven summer heat; a building assessment is needed to avoid promising the same result to every household.
- Right-of-way trees can shade sidewalks and shared outdoor routes regardless of a resident's tenure, but the limited share of near-building sites means they will not directly serve every renter-majority building.
- The panel recommends reporting participation and reach by building size and block, surveying tenants after roof work, and publishing tree survival and establishment-care completion. These measures reveal who benefits without assuming that enrollment alone proves lower indoor heat exposure.
