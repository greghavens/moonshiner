# Urban heat reading inbox

These are personal source cards, not a comprehensive literature review. For
this task, only claims written in the excerpts below count as sourced facts.

## S1 — Block-scale temperature logger pilot

- Publisher: River City Climate Office
- Year: 2024
- Excerpt: Air-temperature loggers were mounted two metres above ground and
  recorded at 15-minute intervals. The pilot ran for 18 summer days in one
  neighborhood west of the river. The card reports no observations from the
  rest of the city and no measurements from another season.

## S2 — Hot nights and sleep diary study

- Authors: Mendez, Okafor, and Lin
- Year: 2023
- Excerpt: Forty-three adults completed self-reported sleep diaries during a
  heat event. Warmer bedrooms were associated with more reported awakenings.
  The authors say the observational design does not establish that heat caused
  the awakenings.

## S3 — Transit shade access audit

- Publisher: Neighborhood Tree Lab
- Year: 2022
- Excerpt: Volunteers audited 12 bus stops between noon and 2 p.m. on one July
  day. Three of the 12 stops had shade covering the marked waiting area. The
  audit did not observe morning, evening, or winter conditions.

## S4 — Cooling-center activation memo

- Publisher: Municipal Preparedness Office
- Year: 2025
- Excerpt: Designated library branches may be activated as cooling centers
  after the city issues a heat alert. The memo names the emergency manager as
  the activation authority. It does not state a funding amount or evaluate the
  branches for disability access.

## S5 — Cool-roof housing pilot

- Publisher: River City Housing Agency
- Year: 2021
- Excerpt: Reflective roofs were installed on 24 occupied apartment buildings.
  During the six-week monitoring window, average afternoon indoor temperature
  was 1.1 degrees Celsius lower than in the matched comparison buildings. The
  card contains no installation-cost data and no follow-up beyond six weeks.

## S6 — East Ward renter interviews

- Publisher: East Ward Tenants Coalition
- Year: 2025
- Excerpt: In eight convenience-sample interviews, renters repeatedly raised
  predictable maintenance access and multilingual heat notices as priorities.
  The coalition cautions that the interviews cannot estimate how common those
  priorities are among all East Ward renters.
