# Source note: American Heart Association

Source label for the final handout: `[AHA]`

Primary page: https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/understanding-blood-pressure-readings/monitoring-your-blood-pressure-at-home

This protected note paraphrases the page's adult home-monitoring guidance and its blood-pressure category chart.

## Monitor and recording guidance

- For home checks, use an automatic monitor with a cuff that goes around the upper arm. A wrist or finger device is not the preferred type in this guidance.
- Home monitoring can help a health care professional assess blood pressure, but it does not replace regular appointments.
- Do not stop taking blood pressure medication because of home readings without first checking with a health care professional.
- Measure at a consistent time. At each measurement session, take two readings separated by one minute and record both results.
- One reading is a snapshot. A record collected over time gives the patient and clinician a more complete picture.

## Adult blood pressure categories

The two numbers use millimeters of mercury, abbreviated `mm Hg`.

| Category | Systolic (upper number) | Relationship | Diastolic (lower number) |
|---|---:|:---:|---:|
| Normal | Less than 120 | and | Less than 80 |
| Elevated | 120–129 | and | Less than 80 |
| Stage 1 hypertension | 130–139 | or | 80–89 |
| Stage 2 hypertension | 140 or higher | or | 90 or higher |

When the two numbers fall into different categories, the `or` logic in the hypertension rows means that the higher category applies. Only a doctor or another qualified medical professional can confirm a diagnosis.

## A reading above 180/120 mm Hg

If a reading is suddenly above 180/120 mm Hg, wait at least one minute and measure again.

- If the repeated reading remains above 180/120 mm Hg and there are no emergency symptoms listed below, contact a health care professional immediately.
- If the reading is above 180/120 mm Hg and it is accompanied by chest pain, shortness of breath, back pain, numbness, weakness, a change in vision, or difficulty speaking, call 911. Do not wait to see whether the pressure falls on its own.

This source does not supply one personalized treatment target for every reader. Monitoring frequency and treatment decisions belong in discussion with the reader's health care professional.
