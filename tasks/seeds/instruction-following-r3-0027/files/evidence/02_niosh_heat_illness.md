# NIOSH guidance digest: recognizing heat illness

Source label for the brief: `[NIOSH Heat Illness]`

This packet digest summarizes NIOSH descriptions of heat-related illnesses and first aid. It is supplied as a source document for this exercise.

Heat exhaustion can include headache, nausea, dizziness, weakness, irritability, thirst, heavy sweating, elevated body temperature, and decreased urine output. Recommended first-aid steps include moving the person to a clinic or emergency room for medical evaluation and treatment, calling 911 if medical care is unavailable, staying with the person until help arrives, removing the person from the hot area, giving liquids to drink, removing unnecessary clothing, and cooling with cold compresses or by washing the head, face, and neck with cold water.

Heat stroke is the most serious heat-related illness. Signs can include confusion, altered mental status, slurred speech, loss of consciousness, hot dry skin or profuse sweating, seizures, and very high body temperature. Heat stroke can cause death or permanent disability without emergency treatment. Call 911 for emergency medical care, move the person to a shaded cool area, cool the person quickly, and do not leave the person alone.

Symptoms can overlap, and this digest does not provide a way for a caregiver to diagnose the type or severity of illness remotely. The listed signs are not a substitute for an individual clinical assessment.
