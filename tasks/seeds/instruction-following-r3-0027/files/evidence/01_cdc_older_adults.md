# CDC guidance digest: heat and older adults

Source label for the brief: `[CDC Older Adults]`

This packet digest summarizes the CDC page “Heat and Older Adults (Aged 65+).” It is supplied as a source document for this exercise.

Adults age 65 and older are more prone to heat-related health problems. Older adults do not adjust as well as younger people to sudden temperature changes. They are also more likely to have a chronic medical condition that changes normal responses to heat and to take prescription medicines that affect the body's ability to control temperature or sweat.

CDC advises people who are caring for an older adult to visit at least twice a day during hot weather and watch for signs of heat stress. A person should drink more water than usual and not wait until thirsty. People whose doctor limits how much they drink or who take water pills should ask their doctor how much to drink while the weather is hot.

Staying in an air-conditioned building is a primary protective step. If a home is not air-conditioned, CDC advises contacting the local health department or locating an air-conditioned shelter. A fan should not be relied on as the main cooling device when it is extremely hot.

This guidance identifies group-level risk factors; it does not predict how a particular person will respond to heat. It does not compare different caregiver check-in schedules, evaluate a combined caregiver program, or identify which local cooling locations are open and accessible.
