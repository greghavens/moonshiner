# National Weather Service digest: heat alerts and heat index

Source label for the brief: `[NWS Heat Tools]`

This packet digest summarizes National Weather Service explanations of heat alerts and the heat index. It is supplied as a source document for this exercise.

The heat index combines air temperature and relative humidity to describe how hot conditions feel to the human body. Standard heat-index values are calculated for shady, light-wind conditions. Exposure to full sunshine can increase heat-index values by up to 15 degrees Fahrenheit. Strong winds can also be dangerous in very hot, dry weather.

National Weather Service offices issue heat watches, warnings, and advisories based on expected local conditions. Alert criteria can vary by location because climate and the effects of heat differ across the country. Forecasts and alerts describe area conditions, not the temperature inside a particular home or a person's individual medical risk.

Warm nights can add risk because the body has less opportunity to recover from daytime heat. Caregivers can monitor the local National Weather Service forecast and alerts, but the packet does not establish a universal heat-index number at which every person needs the same response. It also does not inventory local transportation, cooling-center hours, accessibility, or backup options during an outage.
